# Combat presentation and render budget

Compared with the sibling COD4 checkout on October 16, 2026. The starting
COD2 revision was `3a05a50bc564854ceeecbb0520fa62123a6a5471`.

## What transfers within the existing budget

COD4's `cg_weapons.cpp` submits segmented, potentially wiggling tracer beams.
Its `rb_postfx.cpp` uses downsampled render targets and multiple full-screen
filters for depth of field. COD2's browser renderer uses the fixed-function
DX7 material path. Reproducing COD4's full post-processing, shadow maps and
normal/specular material pipeline would require additional rendering work.
They are not part of this change.

COD2 already had muzzle lights, tracers, fog, baked shadow filtering, contact
shadows, weapon focus, anisotropic filtering, sprint and controller controls.
Those are not new features introduced here.

| Change | Visible or gameplay result | Work budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon clarity | ADS retains the original weapon and hand texture detail, including the rear receiver and sights | One unmodified base sample shared with flash illumination. Removed blur calculations, uniform uploads and viewmodel tracking; no framebuffer, extra texture or screen pass |
| Tracer ribbons | Off-center parallel shots retain a camera-facing width. The muzzle-side fade of this iteration was later removed (see "Tracer visibility") | Same four vertices, material, spawn probability, speed, distance and lifetime; empty beams are skipped |
| Bullet near-miss sound | Disabling tracer visuals no longer suppresses incoming bullet audio | Original sound selection and distance checks; no new default sound instances |
| FX light selection | A nearby muzzle flash or broad explosion can replace a weaker distant light instead of being dropped by arrival order | Four default light slots and the same GPU arrays; bounded CPU ranking at effect submission, without visibility traces |
| Light/shadow handoff | Existing lighting and contact shadows use a shared camera transform cache | One WebAssembly-to-JavaScript call per draw replaces two; inactive channels are invalidated correctly when the view changes |
| Fixed-function texture attributes | Materials retain their UVs without invalid attribute submissions | Respect both sampler and vertex-attribute limits; skip inactive coordinate attributes while retaining texture matrix updates |

The initial material mip approximation was removed after visual feedback: it
discarded texture detail without softening the silhouette or illumination,
making ADS look lower quality. Both hip and ADS now use the authored texture
footprint and existing filtering. This iteration enabled no replacement blur;
the later scene compositor adds a depth-based aim blur after rendering, which
leaves texture detail intact (see "Scene compositor").

COD4's `cg_weapons.cpp` interpolates weapon-specific `adsDofStart/adsDofEnd`
with the aiming fraction. `rb_postfx.cpp` computes viewmodel and scene depth
equations, resolves the rendered scene, downsamples, applies Gaussian/near-CoC
filters and composites it. That is optical image filtering, not a texture mip
downgrade. Reproducing those passes would add GPU work, so this correction
restores sharpness instead of claiming to reproduce COD4 DOF at no cost.

The FX light ranking uses brightness divided by one plus squared distance in
units of the light radius. Ties retain earlier lights. It does not perform
occlusion tests. The normal browser limit remains four; no effect textures,
particle pools, LOD distances, output resolution or gameplay rules are expanded.

## Scene compositor, tracer visibility, mantle camera and command delivery

This iteration started from `755d63e` and compared the sibling COD4 checkout
(KisakCOD IW3) again: its aim depth of field, visible tracers and climbing.

### Scene compositor

`downstream/wasm/web_scene_fx.c` renders the first full-screen 3D view of each
frame into an offscreen RGB8 colour and depth-stencil target. With
`r_aaSamples` above one, the view renders into multisampled renderbuffers that
one blit resolves. Before the first 2D draw the scene is composed into the
canvas; the HUD and later views draw directly. The canvas itself is no longer
multisampled, which would only resolve the composed frame and HUD again.

| Effect | Passes | Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COD2 DX9 glow | Bright pass and one 2D bloom pass, both at 1/8 resolution | `r_glow`, `r_glowBloomIntensity0/1`, `r_glowRadius0/1`, `r_glowBloomCutoff`, `r_glowBloomDesaturation` |
| COD4-style aim depth of field | Blur-size downsample and one 5×5 blur at 1/4 resolution, only while aiming | `r_dof_enable` (new, default 1) |
| Sharpening and colour grade | Part of the full-resolution compose pass | `r_filmTweakEnable` (new, default 1) |

As with COD4's `adsDofStart`/`adsDofEnd`, the aim blur covers the viewmodel
from the eye and scales with the aim fraction. Outside the screen centre, world
pixels bordering the blurred weapon borrow its blur, so the weapon has no sharp
halo. The blur comes from scene depth after rendering; texture resolution is
not reduced, unlike the removed material mip approximation. The focus range and
strength, and the near-world blur, are described under "Softer aim depth of
field" below; they replaced this iteration's first range (0.88 of the muzzle
distance, sharp world).

A frame uses at most five full-screen triangles plus the multisample resolve:
two for glow, two for the aim blur and one for the compose. Without aiming it
uses three. Nothing is read back from the GPU and no queries are issued; the write
masks are not queried either, because reading them stalls on the GPU process.
Under `r_fullbright`, or when glow, grade and aim blur are off and
`r_aaSamples` is 1, the view draws directly to the canvas as before. If the
target cannot be created, the client also falls back to drawing directly.

### Tracer visibility

The previous ribbon faded toward the muzzle. From the shooter's view, most of
the on-screen line is the few units nearest the muzzle, so the fade hid the
shot leaving the barrel. The browser tracer now stays white along its length
like the COD4 beam, uses the centre column of the additive tracer image as a
hot core and adds a small camera-facing glow at the round. That glow is one
more four-vertex polygon per tracer. The browser `cg_tracerlength` default is
240 instead of 200. Locally predicted shots now roll `cg_tracerchance` like
other players' shots; previously each one drew a tracer whenever the chance
was above zero. Carentan captures from the rebuilt client show
the streak in the first frame after a shot (`out/scene-fx-qa/tracer2-*`).

### Mantle camera

`Mantle_Start` moves the player to the start of the authored climb in a single
step. In the browser, `CG_SmoothMantleView` carries the eye across that step
over 200 ms with smoothstep easing. Steps over 48 units, teleports, stalls and
clock resets are not eased. For a stationary mantle in the QA match at
`(70 1062 100)`, yaw 90, the HEAD client's eye dropped from z 60 to 58 within
about 19 ms of the jump; the rebuilt client rose continuously. Both ended at
`(70 1078 91)`.

### Command delivery for jump, stance, aim and sprint

While validating mantles, both HEAD and the pre-fix candidate predicted jumps
and mantles locally that the server then undid. During failed mantles, the
server's `mantle_debug` output reported `Mantle: Found ledge` but no mantle
start.

The cause was `MSG_WriteDeltaUsercmdKey` in `src/PC/qcommon/msg_mp.c`. Only
the long form of a command delta carries the buttons above attack (including
sprint, lean, prone, crouch, jump and aim), the weapon, offhand and roll. The
encoder chose that form only when the time delta exceeded 255 ms, which in
practice means the first command of each packet. A change in a later command
was written in the short form, and the server decoder kept the previous value.
Held buttons therefore reached the server up to one packet late, and presses
released within a packet were lost, while the client had already predicted
them.

The encoder now selects the long form whenever those fields change, regardless
of the time encoding, as KisakCOD's encoder does. The decoder already reads the
form selector independently of the time encoding, so the change is client-only
and existing servers decode it unchanged. Unchanged commands still use the
one-bit shortcut, and commands that change only view angles, attack or
movement still use the short form.

The QA harness presses and releases the jump key at once, so a single command
carries the jump. Jump runs alternated
between the rebuilt client and HEAD; five candidate and then three HEAD mantle
runs followed. Each run used a freshly restarted QA server.

| Run | HEAD `755d63e` | Rebuilt client |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing jump at `(0 1000 100)` | 0 of 5 completed: the eye peaked at z 61–63 about 53 ms after the tap and returned to 52 | 5 of 5 completed, eye apex z 88 |
| Walking mantle at `(70 1062 100)`, yaw 90 | 2 of 3 started on the server; the other was predicted for six client frames and undone, leaving the player at the wall | 5 of 5 started on the server (54–55 `Already mantling` frames each); the eye rose continuously to z 91 |

The shipped `kbitmask[18]` is `0x7ffff`, so the decoder can set the unused
button bit 19 when the 18-bit button field changes. No code reads that bit.

## Softer aim depth of field (October 17, 2026)

After playing the previous build, the user found the aim blur too strong and
limited to the weapon and arm. In the reference they sent (Call of Duty: WWII),
the gun is blurred, the hand is slightly sharper and the nearby ground along
the bottom of the view is blurred too. COD4's `CG_UpdateAdsDof` also blurs the
near world while aiming.

### Focus

While aiming, `CG_UpdateWebWeaponPresentation` ports COD4's focus rules. One
trace runs 8192 units along the view axis with the bullet content mask,
skipping the local player. The world blurs from one unit up to `nearEnd`,
which stays 30 units short of the nearest other player around the screen
(clamped to 50–512 units) and of the surface under the crosshair (at least one
unit). With nobody on screen COD4 uses 256 units; the browser uses 512, so the
ground along the bottom of the view softens while the street beyond stays
sharp. Once fully aimed in, the focus eases like COD4's `CG_UpdateAdsDofValue`:
half the remaining distance per frame, at least one unit and at most 50 units
per 50 ms. The player scan reads the 64 client entities already in
`cg_entities`; the trace is the only new collision query, once per frame while
aiming.

### Blur

The compositor reconstructs world distance from the view's infinite
projection: `WebSceneFx_BeginView` receives `-projectionMatrix[14]`, which is
0.9995 × `r_znear`. Like COD4's `r_dof_bias` of 0.5, both circles of confusion
are squared, so partly defocused surfaces blur much less than the nearest
ones. The largest blur radius is 3 (480-line pixels) instead of 6. The three
blur images now switch where each matches its fraction of the largest blur at
the current resolution, instead of at the fixed fractions 0.25 and 0.55.

A depth probe in the Carentan QA match read, with the Thompson aimed in:
receiver 5.0–5.7 units, rear sight 6.0–6.5, trigger hand 9.5–9.6, forearm
sleeve 13.4–16.9 and front sight 27.5–28. The previous range (0.88 of the
muzzle distance, clamped to 12.5–19.5 units, starting at 0.4 of the end) left
the trigger hand nearly as blurred as the receiver. The viewmodel is now sharp
from 0.625 of the muzzle distance, clamped to 10–17.5 units, and starts
blurring at 0.26 of that distance. For the Thompson the running client reported
4.55–17.5 units.

No pass was added: aiming still uses the two quarter-resolution passes, and the
compose pass evaluates the world blur from the depth it already fetches.

## Ejected shell casings (October 17, 2026)

The user asked for spent casings flying out to the side while firing, as in
Call of Duty: WWII. CoD2 already does this. With `cg_brass 1` (the default),
`CG_EjectWeaponBrass` plays the weapon's shell eject effect at the viewmodel's
`tag_brass` for every shot; bolt-action rifles eject on `EV_EJECT_BRASS`. The
effects in `fx/shellejects/` each hold one physics emitter carrying a casing
model: `pistol.efx` (`xmodel/prop_pistol_shell`, 10 seconds, within 250 units
of the camera), `rifle.efx` (5 seconds, 100 units) and `shotgun.efx`.

### Why no casing appeared

A temporarily instrumented build showed every step working up to the draw. The
Thompson ejected from viewmodel handle 1032, bone 72, about 12 units from the
eye. The emitter was created in world space (no bolt), and its physics moved
it each frame. `Emitter_Draw` then skipped it, because `mRefEnt.scale` was 0:
`Emitter_Update` set only `mRefEnt.radius[0]` from the size curve and never
set the scale or the model's axis.

### Casing motion

`Emitter_Update` now ends with `Emitter_UpdateModel`, which ports Raven's
`CEmitter::UpdateAngles`. The angles advance by the effect's angle delta in
degrees per 100 ms (`angleDelta` is ±233 in the shell ejects). Once the casing
stops moving, the spin dies down by a factor of 0.7 per 16 ms, applied per
millisecond so the frame rate does not change it. The axis comes from those
angles, and the model draws at the size curve's value, which is 1 (the
model's own size) in all three shell ejects. Under `fx_freeze` (no elapsed
time) a casing holds still and keeps drawing.

### Renderer bug exposed by the casings

Before this change, no loose model (`reType` 1) had reached the renderer.
`R_UpdateXModelBounds` set the shared model DObj but then passed the `XModel`
itself as the DObj. `DObjGetBoneInfo` then read garbage model and bone counts
and wrote past its 128-entry array on the stack. In the browser this
corrupted the camera: both runs with visible casings started with the view
pitched about 45° up. The `aec62a1` build, the build before this change and a
run with `fx_draw 0` all started level. The function now uses the shared DObj,
as `R_GetGfxEntityDObj` does, and the next run started level.

### Culling

`Emitter_Cull` returned 0, so each casing on the ground was drawn every frame,
even out of view. It now uses the same frustum sphere test as the other
primitives. The sphere is centred on the model origin; its radius is the
model's largest corner distance from `mins`/`maxs` times the scale, so it holds
the model in any orientation. `fx_cull 0` still disables culling.

The same draw path also restores the models of the other model emitters in
the effect files:
- wood splinters from `fx/impacts/large_woodhit.efx` and
  `fx/explosions/grenadeexp_wood.efx`;
- barrel shards from `fx/props/barrelexp.efx`;
- Flak 88 pieces from `fx/explosions/flak88_explosion.efx`;
- the burnt grenade left by `fx/props/american_smoke_grenade.efx`.

### Evidence

Captures used the Thompson at `setviewpos 0 1000 100 0` in the Carentan QA
match, at 1280×720 in Chrome/ANGLE on an Apple M4. At full speed a casing
spinning out to the right of the gun appears in some of the 60 ms frames.
With `timescale 0.15`, consecutive frames show one casing per shot leaving the
ejection port to the right and falling while it turns, with its base and
primer lit.

For performance, each build ran two alternating uncapped rounds in one session

## Hit marker (October 17, 2026)

The user asked for the Call of Duty: WWII hit feedback. In their capture the

### What CoD2 did

- The crosshair turned red while the server flagged an enemy under it within

  then sets the target `color`. `CoD2MP_s.exe` (0x4dd791–0x4dd833) computes

  not have reached existing players, whose configs save it.
- `CG_Draw2dHudElems` draws the `damage_feedback` element as the hit marker:

  the same in wasm32, and the loop also runs on 64-bit test hosts.

## Minimap (October 17, 2026)

The user asked for the Call of Duty: WWII minimap in place of CoD2's compass:
a round map in the top-left corner that turns with the player and shows the
real layout. Their capture shows dark streets and paths between tan blocks
with dark outlines, a white triangle for the player at the center pointing
up, teammates as cyan triangles and an orange marker. CoD2 ships no top-down
images of its maps, so the client builds one when the level loads.

### Building the map

`CG_MinimapInit`, called from `CG_Init`, builds the map once per level from
the collision world:

- The area is the drawn world (non-sky surfaces; skyboxes reach far past the
  playable space), within the collision bounds and within 1,536 units of the
  box around the spawn points.
- A texel covers `max(12, longest side / 400)` units; the texture is at most
  512×512.
- Each texel traces straight down against solid, glass and water from above
  the highest drawn surface. Sky hits and misses are left empty.
- The ground level is a grey-scale opening of those heights (erosion, then
  dilation, over 768 units). It removes whatever stands above its
  surroundings and is narrower than 1,536 units.
- The walkable surface is flood-filled from the spawn points that stand on the
  traced surface (within 40 units). The fill crosses to a neighbor whose
  height differs by at most 20 units plus 0.6 texels and whose slope is
  walkable. Unreached texels within 24 units of the ground level, such as
  closed courtyards and terraces, are ground too; the rest are buildings.
  Building pieces under 1,024 square units, such as posts and crates, become
  ground.
- Ground takes its color from the surface type: grass is darker, dirt, gravel,
  mud and sand are lighter, so paths stand out, paved roads are grey, and
  snow, wood floors and water have their own tones. Buildings are tan with a
  darker outline. They cast a short shadow to the south-east, and slopes are
  lit from the north-west.

The pixels stay in memory (1 MiB at 512×512), so after a video restart the
texture is uploaded again without new traces. Building uses about 18 bytes per
texel (at most 4.7 MiB), freed afterwards.

### Drawing

- The compass ownerdraws draw the minimap instead. It is a disc of
  `cg_minimapSize` virtual units (default 120) at 6, 6 from the top-left
  corner. The distance from its center to its edge is `cg_minimapRange` world
  units (default 1,400). Forward is up.
- The player is a cream arrow at the center. Teammates are cyan arrows showing
  where they face, from the same data as the compass, and flash as they do
  there. Enemies who fire show as red dots where they fired, fading over
  `compassPingFadeTime`. Objectives use their own icons (CTF flags, bomb sites
  A and B, HQ radios) and follow the entity they are attached to. Markers out
  of range stay on the edge.
- The disc has an antialiased edge and a soft shadow ring, and fades with the
  compass (`hud_fade_compass`).
- While the minimap is shown, the chat, the say field, the kill feed and the
  top-left script HUD elements that start left of its right edge, such as the
  clock and the scores, move down by its size plus the margin, 126 virtual
  units by default. Free spectators and intermission do not paint the
  compass, so nothing moves there.
- `cg_minimap 0` restores the compass and the original positions. The three
  dvars are saved in the profile. If the map texture cannot be created, the
  compass stays.

### Renderer additions

- Render command 6, an unused slot, draws indexed 2D triangles with a
  position, texture coordinates and color per vertex, in the same 2D batch as
  stretch pics. A command takes up to 1,024 vertices and 3,072 indices.
- HUD image materials copy `$raw`, the alpha-blended 2D material, with their
  own image, linear filtering, no mipmaps and clamped addressing. They are
  released when the renderer shuts down.

sent an objective without a team only to players without a team, and a team's
objective to everyone. CTF flags at base, Search and Destroy bomb sites and an

sets it when a flag returns home, and the SD bomb sites never get a team. Now

### Icons

A weapon's kill icon is its HUD icon, shaded grey: the visible texels of the
M1 Garand, Kar98k, Thompson, MP40, MP44 and Colt icons have a median luma of
0.44 to 0.51, so a tinted icon looked dim. The renderer builds a flat copy
instead (`R_GetHudSilhouetteMaterial` in `r_hud2d.c`):

- The first time an icon is needed, it reads the image's IWi file and decodes
  the top mip of DXT3 or DXT5 (IWi version 5, at most 128 texels a side) into
  white texels that keep the alpha. The top mip is the last one in the file.
- The image is trimmed to the texels above alpha 16 plus one texel and
  uploaded as a single mip, drawn through a copy of `$raw` with linear
  filtering and clamped addressing.
- The feed fits a silhouette into 44×13 in its own proportions, so a rifle is
  long and thin and a pistol short. If an image cannot be decoded, the stock
  icon is drawn in a 22-unit box, twice as wide for wide icons.
- The cache holds 64 images and remembers failures, so each file is read at
  most once per renderer start. It is emptied when the renderer shuts down.

### Obituary fixes

- The reconstructed client mapped the special means of death to the wrong
  icons: headshots showed the crush icon, crush deaths the headshot icon and
  suicides the generic death icon. In `CoD2MP_s.exe` the obituary subtracts 7
  from the means of death and jumps through the table at 0x4e078c: melee (7),
  headshot (8), crush (9), falling (11) and suicide (12) have their icons, and
  anything else uses the generic death icon. The client now follows that
  table.
- A headshot's event carries the headshot means of death instead of the
  weapon. The native server adds the weapon as a fourth argument to the
  reliable `cod2_obituary` broadcast, and the browser shows that weapon with
  the headshot mark. Without it, as from an older server or in the native
  client, which reads the entity event, the stock headshot icon fills the
  slot. A client that accepts only three arguments drops headshot
  obituaries.
- The weapon does not travel in a command of its own, because clients
